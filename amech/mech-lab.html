The Ledger of Balance and the Twinbell Toll

In the Counting House of Fulcrum Keep, two iron weights sit on a long balance-beam. The heavier weight, Anchor's Burden (m1), rests at one marked position — its Anchor's Post (x1) — while the lighter weight, Second Burden (m2), rests at its own marked spot, the Second Post (x2).

Somewhere between the two, weighted toward whichever burden is heavier, sits the beam's true balance point — the Fulcrum's Heart (xCOM). Move Anchor's Burden further from Second Burden's post, or make it heavier still, and the Fulcrum's Heart creeps toward it.

Beyond the ledger hall, two carts race toward the same crossing. Each rolls in at its own Approach Speed (u1, u2) — how fast Anchor's cart and Second's cart are moving before they meet. When they collide and lock together, both carts lurch onward as one at a single shared Afterspeed (v) — slower than either approached alone, since the heavier cart drags the lighter one down to its pace, and the lighter one drags the heavier one up.

Whatever a single cart carries while rolling — its mass moving at its speed — is its Rolling Weight (p). A sudden shove from Fulcrum Keep's ram changes a cart's Rolling Weight by some amount — the Toll Shift (Δp) — delivered over some Toll's Ring (Δt), the time the ram is actually in contact. The harder the ram pushes — the Ram's Push (F) — the bigger the Toll Shift for the same Toll's Ring; the longer the Toll's Ring, the gentler the same Ram's Push can be for the same Toll Shift.

The Ring-Bound Sentinel and the Twin Worlds

In the Ringworks of Wandering Hold, a sentinel golem of fixed weight — the Bound Mass (m) — circles endlessly around the Hold's central spire, never straying inward or outward, held forever on its path.

The golem sweeps around its circle at a steady pace — the Circling Speed (v) — never slowing, never rushing, forever tracing the same loop.

From the spire's heart to the golem's own path lies a fixed distance — the Tether's Reach (r) — the radius of its endless circuit.

Binding the golem inward, stopping it flying off into the wild beyond, is an unseen pull toward the spire — the Sentinel's Chain (Fc) — invisible, but strong enough to hold a circling weight on its course.

The spire itself has its own vast bulk — the Spire's Weight (M) — far greater than the golem's own, its gravity alone enough to command an orbit.

Every gravity-binding in the Hold, from the smallest pebble to the mightiest spire, obeys the same fixed universal number woven into the world's making — the Warden's Constant (G) — unchanging, whatever the scale.

And the true pull holding golem to spire, reckoned from the Warden's Constant, the Spire's Weight, the Bound Mass and the Tether's Reach, is the Spire's Grasp (Fg) — the very same gravity that binds a falling apple, just far, far stronger here.

The Great Millwheel of Cogturn Hollow

Deep in Cogturn Hollow turns a vast stone millwheel. As it spins, it sweeps through an angle — the Swept Angle (Δθ) — over some stretch of time, the Turning Span (Δt), giving the wheel its steady spinning pace, the Turning Rate (ω).

The millwheel doesn't always spin at the same rate — sometimes the miller lets it speed up or slow down, a change measured by the Turning Surge (α).

How stubbornly the wheel resists any change to its spin — heavier at the rim, harder to speed up or slow — is its Turning Stubbornness (I). A wheel built with its mass spread wide at the rim resists far more than one built light and close to the axle.

To overcome that stubbornness and actually change the wheel's spin, the miller must apply a twist at the axle — the Miller's Twist (τ) — greater than the wheel's own resistance, or nothing changes at all.

And once spinning, the wheel carries a certain rotational momentum of its own — the Millwheel's Whirl (L) — built from how stubborn it is to change and how fast it's already turning. Stop the miller's twist entirely, and the Millwheel's Whirl carries the wheel on turning, undiminished, forever.

The Coilwrought Bellows of Tidewell Vault

In Tidewell Vault hangs a great iron bellows from a coiled spring. How the bellows bounces depends on two things: its own heft, the Bellows' Burden (m), and the coil's own stubborn stiffness, the Coil's Grip (k) — a stiffer coil or a lighter bellows means a quicker bounce.

Each full bounce — out, back, and returning to where it started, moving the same way — takes some measured stretch of time, the Bellows' Round (T).

At any moment the bellows sits some distance from its resting hang-point, the Sway (y) — swinging first one way, then the other, through that resting point and back again.

The greater the Sway, the harder the coil hauls the bellows back toward its resting point — a pull called the Coil's Recall (F) — always aimed back toward rest, never away from it.

Stretch or compress the coil and it stores away a hidden reserve, ready to be spent — the Coil's Hoard (Ep) — greatest when the Sway is greatest, and spent entirely into motion as the bellows swings back through its resting point.

Conservation of momentum

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁+m₂)v

Used for collisions where two objects stick together (perfectly inelastic). Built from p = mv (which IS on the sheet) applied to each object, plus the law that total momentum before a collision equals total momentum after — that law itself isn't printed anywhere, you're expected to know it.
