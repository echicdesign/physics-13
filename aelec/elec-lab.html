The Rune-Smith's Forge of Emberhold

In the Rune-Smith's Forge of Emberhold, a steady stream of mana — the Flowspark (I) — is driven down a rune-wire toward the anvil. Every rune-wire resists that flow to some degree — its Choke-Ward (R) — some wires barely slow the mana at all, others throttle it to a trickle.

The pressure the forge's crystal battery must build to force the Flowspark through a given Choke-Ward is the Surge-Pressure (V) — push a strong Flowspark through a stubborn Choke-Ward, and the Surge-Pressure climbs.

However fast the forge burns mana into heat and light is its Burn-Rate (P) — the fiercer the Flowspark, or the tighter the Choke-Ward, the faster the forge burns.

Keep the forge running for some stretch of time — the Forge-Span (t) — and the mana burned over that whole stretch piles up into the Ember-Hoard (W), spent entirely into heat and light.

And however many individual sparks have marched down the rune-wire in that same stretch of time is the Spark-Tally (Q) — the running count of mana particles that have passed the anvil so far.

The Rune-Chain of Wandering Staff

A wizard's staff carries a single unbroken rune-wire, strung with three rune-crystals in a row — each one a Chain-Link (R1, R2, R3) resisting the mana a little, one after another, so that every scrap of mana forced through the first must also pass through the second, then the third.

Because there is only one path, the same Flowspark threads through every crystal in turn — and the whole chain behaves as if it were one single, larger resistance: the Full Chain-Ward (Rtotal), simply the sum of every Chain-Link laid end to end.

As the Flowspark forces its way past each crystal, it spends a little of the battery's push at every one — a Toll (V1, V2, V3) paid to each crystal in turn, bigger for a stubborner crystal.

And however the tolls are split between the three crystals, they always add back up to the crystal battery's own full push — the Grand Toll (Vtotal) — not a drop of pressure is lost along the chain.

The Amulet of Three Channels

An enchantress's amulet splits a single rune-wire into three separate channels, side by side, each rejoining the wire further along — three Side-Paths (R1, R2, R3) a current can choose between, each resisting the mana by its own amount.

Because all three paths reconnect at both ends, the same Surge-Pressure pushes across every one of them at once — and mana always finds the easiest of the three Side-Paths easiest to flow down, splitting itself between them according to how stubborn each one is: three separate Path-Flows (I1, I2, I3).

Whatever splits between the three Side-Paths must add back up to the amulet's Grand Flow (Itotal) — the full mana-current the crystal battery has to supply to feed all three at once.

And however easy the three Side-Paths are individually, opening up more of them always makes the amulet's overall Channel-Ward (Rtotal) smaller than any single Side-Path alone — more doors open, less overall resistance to getting through.
