The Cartographer of the Arcane Plane

In the Sunken Archive of Glyphwater, every rune is drawn on a single flat plane. Step east or west along the floor and you trace the rune's Eastward Mark (a) — how far the rune sits from the Archive's centre along the real, everyday direction.

Step instead toward the Archive's shimmering back wall — a direction no ordinary compass can find — and you trace the rune's Otherward Mark (b), its position along the imaginary axis.

Together, the Eastward Mark and the Otherward Mark pin a rune to one exact point on the plane, its rectangular coordinates.

However far that rune sits from the Archive's centre, measured in a straight line rather than along either wall, is its True Distance (|z|) — the modulus. A rune pinned far east and far otherward still has one single, honest distance back to the centre.

The dot shows z = a + bi. The dashed line shows |z|, its straight-line distance from the origin.

The Bearing-Keeper of Glyphwater

Not every cartographer in Glyphwater walks in straight east-and-otherward steps. The Bearing-Keeper instead marks each rune two other ways: how far it sits from the Archive's centre, its Reach (r), and which direction to walk to find it, its Bearing (θ), measured as an angle swung round from due east.

A rune with a longer Reach sits further out on the plane. A rune with a Bearing of zero sits due east; swing further round, and the Bearing grows.

Give the Bearing-Keeper both the Reach and the Bearing, and they can walk straight to any rune on the plane without ever counting east-and-otherward steps at all — polar form and rectangular form are just two different ways of pointing at the exact same spot.

The arrow shows z at distance r from the origin, at angle theta from the positive real axis.
